## Things to say — and things not to

**Say plainly, early:**

- "We moved from IFS Progress to IFS Food v8 during 2026. Here is our internal audit against the
  full v8 checklist from 13–14 August, and here is what it found." *Volunteering your own findings
  is the single strongest credibility move available.*
- "Our January 2026 recall — here is the notification to Quality Austria within one day, the FSA
  alert, the three-language consumer notices and the Statement of Destruction." *Then be honest
  that the root cause analysis is not yet closed; the auditor will find that anyway.*
- "Several records live in Odoo rather than the Drive. I can show them live."

**Do not:**

- Do not offer *"Notranja presoja, januar 2026"* as an internal audit. It is the IFS self-assessment
  gap tracker, and presenting it as an audit invites a finding on top of a finding.
- Do not hand over the `1.2.4` process register — it cites document codes that do not exist and
  reads as a sheet of scripted answers.
- Do not describe the system using v7 language ("chapter 6 food defence"). In v8, food defence is
  **4.21** and there is no chapter 6.
- Do not claim `4.21.2`'s external-inspection element is N/A without first confirming the US export
  / FDA position — the doctrine ties the exemption to exactly that.
- Do not present any record created in the last fortnight as evidence of a long-running control.
  The auditor sees creation dates.

## If the auditor finds a credibility problem

Three records will not survive scrutiny (identical daily probe values, a 2027 date in OBR20,
−18 °C in a 2–8 °C chiller log). If any is raised, the only correct response is to concede
immediately, log it in OBR22 during the audit, and explain the fix. Defending them costs the whole
sample.
